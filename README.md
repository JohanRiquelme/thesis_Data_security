Balancing Anonymity and Accuracy
An Ethical Assessment of Privacy-Preserving Methods

Johanna del Pilar Riquelme Arias


A Thesis Submitted in Partial Fulfilment 
of the requirements for the 
Degree of 
Master of Science in Data Analytics

Abstract; 


This research was inspired by the Cybersecurity Summer Diploma Program at CCT College, which deepened my understanding of modern data protection practices and legal frameworks, particularly the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). (Commission, 2016)
With a professional background in IT and license administration—including participation in the Y2K readiness initiative—I approached this study with a strong appreciation for the evolution of data integrity and security challenges. This thesis explores the protection of personal and sensitive data, with a focus on the GDPR’s impact on data handling and access in academic research and data science.
The aim is to support future students and professionals in navigating privacy constraints while conducting meaningful analysis. By applying scientific methods and data science techniques, this study demonstrates how data can be protected, responsibly manipulated, and legally analyzed.
The methodology includes reviewing GDPR principles, identifying appropriate datasets, and testing privacy-preserving techniques using established libraries. The U.S. Census Bureau’s Adult Income Dataset (Repository, 1996) was selected for its accessibility and relevance to privacy-focused research.
This work contributes to a broader understanding of data protection strategies under GDPR and offers a practical framework for handling sensitive information in research environments.
